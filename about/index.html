Statement

My painting practice has gradually shifted from figuration towards abstraction. Although the figure remains an underlying point of reference, I eventually found that working figuratively often reinforced my perfectionist tendencies. This led me to seek a process that offered greater freedom and space for intuitive expression. Earlier themes of identity, belonging, duality, self-worth, and female sexuality are still present but I have become less interested in communicating specific narratives and more interested in what can emerge through the act of painting itself.

This inquiry is reflected in my approach to materials and process. After experimenting with numerous painting surfaces over the years, looking for the right balance between absorbency, texture, colour and longevity while retaining a degree of organic quality, paper has become an important collaborator. Rather than treating stains, creases and incidental marks from the studio as imperfections, I allow them to become part of the painting's visual history, sometimes redirecting the composition in unexpected ways. This has led me to consider uncertainty not as something to overcome, but as a generative condition.

My practice is also informed in part by an idea from my mindfulness studies: that two seemingly opposing truths can exist simultaneously without cancelling one another out. Through intention and accident, control and surrender, presence and absence, I explore what happens when opposing forces don’t need to resolve each other but rather what becomes possible when they are allowed to coexist.

My influences span both historical and contemporary painting. I continue to look to Egon Schiele for his expressive intensity. More recently, Martha Jungwirth's approach to painting, particularly her negotiation of chance and impulse, and Lorienne Lotz's fluidity between figuration and abstraction through masterful paint handling, have become important points of reference.

Bio

Olivia Seay grew up in Syracuse, New York, and left in 2001 to pursue her undergraduate studies at Colorado College, graduating in 2005. She subsequently moved to the Pacific Northwest, where she lived until 2008, when she accepted a volunteer position teaching art in South Africa.

After volunteering on the South Coast, Olivia moved to Durban, where she lived until relocating to San Francisco to study in 2013. Later that year, she returned to Durban, where she remained until 2017, when she moved back to the United States. Her return was difficult and initiated a period of introspection, during which she established a meditation practice while on a trip to India. It was through meditation that she reconnected with her longstanding interest in drawing and painting.

In 2018, she attended a figure-drawing course in Italy led by a German artist. The following year, she relocated to Wiesbaden, Germany, to assist the artist with figure-drawing and portraiture courses. She visited Cape Town over the holiday break, intending to resume the arrangement in Germany in the new year. The onset of the COVID-19 pandemic interrupted those plans, and she remained in South Africa.

Olivia now maintains an independent painting practice from her home studio in Muizenberg, working primarily in oil on paper.
