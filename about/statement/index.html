Statement

Over the years my painting practice has gradually shifted from figuration toward abstraction. While the figure remains an underlying point of reference, I found that working figuratively often reinforced my perfectionist tendencies, prompting me to seek a process with greater freedom for intuitive expression. Earlier themes of identity, belonging, duality, self-worth, and female sexuality remain present, though I am now less interested in communicating specific narratives than in discovering what emerges through the act of painting itself.

This shift is closely tied to my materials. After experimenting with numerous surfaces to find the right balance of absorbency, texture, color, and longevity, paper has become an essential collaborator. Rather than treating studio stains, creases, and incidental marks as flaws, I allow them to become part of a painting’s visual history—sometimes redirecting the composition in unexpected ways. Through this process, I have come to view uncertainty not as a condition to overcome, but as a generative space.

My approach is also shaped by a principle from my mindfulness studies: that two seemingly opposing truths can exist simultaneously without canceling each other out. Navigating intention and accident, control and surrender, presence and absence, I explore what becomes possible when these forces are allowed to coexist rather than forced to resolve.

My influences span both historical and contemporary painting. I continue to look to Egon Schiele for his expressive intensity, while Martha Jungwirth’s negotiation of chance and impulse gave me permission to embrace oil on paper. Closer to home, Cape Town-based artist Lorienne Lotz’s raw yet delicate paint handling remains an important point of reference as she moves between figuration and abstraction.
